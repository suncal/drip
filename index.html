Drip

Drop in a bank or card statement and see every recurring charge you're paying — including the ones that quietly went up in price, the ones you forgot about, and what's due to bill next.

Runs entirely in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent anywhere.

How do I get a statement file from my bank?

Nearly every bank has an export button. Look for Download, Export or Statements in your online banking, then choose CSV (sometimes called “spreadsheet”, “Excel” or “Quicken”).

  • Pick the longest range you can — 12 to 24 months is ideal, because yearly subscriptions only show up once.
  • Export your current account and your credit cards, then drop them all in together. Subscriptions hide on the card you look at least.
  • PDF statements won't work. If CSV isn't offered, OFX/QFX (“Quicken format”) will do.

How it works

Drip groups your transactions by merchant — which means untangling descriptors like SQ *BLUE BOTTLE 8452 and PAYPAL *SPOTIFY USA into the actual businesses behind them. It then looks at the gaps between charges from each merchant and measures how regular they are, using the median gap and the median deviation from it rather than an average. That matters: a single skipped month or a payment posted two days late throws an average badly off, while leaving this measurement almost unchanged — and real subscriptions skip, retry and post late all the time.

Anything regular enough gets a cadence, a predicted next charge date and a confidence score. Prices are then checked for step changes that stick, so a genuine rise is separated from a one-off partial or refunded month.

Your data

Everything happens inside this page. There is no server, no account, no analytics and no network request of any kind — you can disconnect from the internet and it will work exactly the same. Close the tab and nothing remains.

Drip is a reading aid, not financial advice, and it can only see what's in the files you give it. Always check a charge against your actual statement before cancelling anything.
